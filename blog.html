Banana fibre is a natural cellulosic fibre extracted from the pseudostem of the banana plant (Musa species). The pseudostem is the thick, trunk-like stalk of the banana plant that remains after the fruit is harvested. Traditionally, this stem was considered agricultural waste and was either burned or left to decompose in the field. At Maleema, we rescue these pseudostems from partner farms across India and process them into premium textile-grade fibre through a mechanical decortication process that requires no chemicals and generates zero additional agricultural footprint. Key Properties of Banana Fibre • Tensile strength: 2× stronger than jute, comparable to bamboo fibre • Natural lustre: silk-like sheen without chemical treatment • Moisture wicking: absorbs and releases moisture efficiently • Naturally antimicrobial: no chemical finishing required for medical or hygiene applications • Biodegradable: fully composts in under 2 years with zero residue • Low water footprint: uses the water already allocated to banana fruit cultivation How Is Banana Fibre Made? The production process at Maleema follows five core steps: • Step 1 — Collect: Pseudostems are collected from partner farms immediately after fruit harvest. No new crops are planted. • Step 2 — Extract: Mechanical decortication separates long cellulosic fibres from the stem. No chemicals are used. • Step 3 — Grade: Fibres are sun-dried and sorted into 12+ grades based on fineness and tensile strength. • Step 4 — Spin: Cottonised banana fibre is spun into yarn, or wet-laid into cellulose sheets. • Step 5 — Fabric: Yarn is woven or knit into finished fabric ready for brand production. What Products Can Be Made from Banana Fibre? Banana fibre is versatile across product categories: • Apparel: T-shirts, kurtas, activewear, innerwear • Home textiles: Upholstery, cushion covers, curtains, rugs • Medical textiles: PPE kits, surgical drapes, wound dressings • Industrial: Rope, geotextiles, composite reinforcement • Packaging: Banana paper, wrapping sheets, stationery Why Are Textile Brands Switching to Banana Fibre?
